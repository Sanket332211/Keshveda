The business problem
We run a D2C haircare brand that sells on our own website (Shopify). About 55% of our
orders are Cash on Delivery (COD).
The problem: around 28% of COD orders are refused at the door or never delivered. This
is called RTO (Return to Origin). For every RTO we lose:
Forward shipping: ₹65
Return shipping: ₹65
Packaging + handling: ₹25
Around 8% of returned products come back damaged and can't be resold (average
product cost ₹180)
Prepaid orders have an RTO rate of only about 4%.
We ship about 3,000 orders a month. Average order value is ₹649. Our gross margin on a
delivered order is about 62%.
We want an AI-powered system that looks at every COD order before it is shipped and
decides what to do with it.
What two of our leaders are saying
The two people you'd work with disagree. Read both carefully.
Head of Operations:
"RTO is killing us. I want any risky COD order cancelled automatically. If a pincode has
bad history, just stop COD there. I'd rather lose a few sales than keep burning money
on returns."

Head of Marketing:
"We spend ₹300+ to acquire each new customer through Meta ads. If we cancel their
first order, we've wasted that money and they'll never come back. Most first-time
buyers choose COD because they don't trust us yet. Please don't kill growth to save
shipping costs."
You don't have to pick a side. But your solution has to deal with both concerns, and you
must explain your reasoning in money (₹), not opinions.
What you need to build
Part 1 — The decision engine (core)
Using the dataset we share ( orders_sample.csv , about 2,000 past COD orders with
whether they were delivered or returned), build a system that takes a new COD order and
outputs one of these actions:
1. Ship as-is
2. Ship after confirmation (customer must confirm on WhatsApp first)
3. Nudge to prepaid (offer a small discount to pay online)
4. Hold / cancel
Requirements:
Use an LLM where it actually helps (for example: judging whether an address is
complete and real, spotting fake names like "asdf" or "test", reading Hinglish
addresses). Do not use an LLM for things simple code or maths can do better. Tell us
where you drew that line and why.
Every decision must come with a short, plain-English reason that an operations person
can understand.
The thresholds (when to cancel vs. confirm vs. nudge) must be easy for a non-technical
person to change, without touching code.
Part 2 — The WhatsApp confirmation flow
Design (and build a working mock of) the message flow for "Ship after confirmation" and
"Nudge to prepaid":
What message goes out, in what language, and when
What happens if the customer replies "yes", "no", something random, or doesn't reply
at all in 24 hours
